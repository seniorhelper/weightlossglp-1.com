Published Clinical Trials

What GLP-1 Actually Does

These aren't before-and-after photos. They're peer-reviewed randomized controlled trials published in the New England Journal of Medicine.

The Clinical Trial Data

For decades, the best available weight-loss medications produced roughly 5–8% body weight loss on average. In large published trials, GLP-1 receptor agonists went well beyond that benchmark, with semaglutide averaging about 15% and tirzepatide about 21% at the highest dose.

These medicines do more than lower the number on the scale. In trials they also improved blood sugar control, blood pressure and cholesterol, and the SELECT trial showed a meaningful reduction in major cardiovascular events with semaglutide.

In every one of these trials, the medicine was given alongside a reduced-calorie diet and increased physical activity. Outcomes depend on the whole plan, not the prescription alone, which is why ongoing follow-up with a licensed clinician matters.

Not sure which medicine fits your situation? Talk to a licensed clinician about your health history, current medications and goals before starting any GLP-1 medicine.

FDA-Approved GLP-1 Medicines

GLP-1 Weight Loss Medications Explained

Two active ingredients make up the FDA-approved GLP-1 options most people hear about. All of them require a prescription from a licensed clinician.

GLP-1 Receptor Agonist
Semaglutide
Brands: Wegovy · Ozempic · Rybelsus

Wegovy is the once-weekly injection approved for chronic weight management. Ozempic (weekly injection) and Rybelsus (daily tablet) are approved for type 2 diabetes. STEP-1 trial: ~15% average weight loss with semaglutide 2.4 mg.

  • Mimics the natural GLP-1 hormone
  • Dose is increased gradually over weeks
  • Studied for heart outcomes in SELECT
  • Prescription required
Dual GLP-1 + GIP Agonist
Tirzepatide
Brands: Zepbound · Mounjaro

Zepbound is the once-weekly injection approved for chronic weight management. Mounjaro is approved for type 2 diabetes. Tirzepatide acts on both GLP-1 and GIP receptors. SURMOUNT-1 trial: ~21% average weight loss at the highest dose.

  • Acts on two gut-hormone receptors
  • Dose is increased gradually over weeks
  • Once-weekly injection
  • Prescription required
How They Work
The Mechanism
Brain, stomach and pancreas

GLP-1 is a hormone your gut releases after eating. These medicines amplify that signal, which reduces hunger, slows digestion and helps regulate blood sugar.

  • Signals fullness to the hypothalamus
  • Slows gastric emptying
  • Glucose-dependent insulin release
  • Suppresses excess glucagon
Before You Start

Side Effects & Cost Basics

Two of the most common questions people have about GLP-1 weight loss medicines.

Common Side Effects

The most common side effects are gastrointestinal: nausea, constipation and occasional vomiting, particularly in the first 2–4 weeks and after dose increases. These typically improve with continued use.

Slow dose titration, starting at the lowest dose and increasing gradually over weeks, is the main strategy for minimizing early side effects. Serious adverse effects are rare but include a risk of pancreatitis.

A licensed prescriber should go over your full medical history and current medications for contraindications before prescribing.

Cost & Insurance

Brand-name GLP-1 medicines are expensive. Without insurance, list prices for brands such as Wegovy and Zepbound have commonly been in the range of $900–$1,300 per month.

Coverage varies widely. Some plans cover GLP-1 medicines for type 2 diabetes but not for weight management, and many require prior authorization.

  • Ask your insurer whether weight-management use is covered
  • Ask whether prior authorization is required
  • Confirm the pharmacy filling the prescription is state-licensed
Getting Care Safely

Finding a Licensed Prescriber

GLP-1 medicines require a prescription. Here is what to look for, what to ask and what to avoid.

How to Find a Licensed Prescriber

Your primary care clinician is a good place to start. Obesity medicine specialists, endocrinologists and licensed telehealth practices can also evaluate whether a GLP-1 medicine is appropriate.

Whoever prescribes should be licensed in your state, take a full medical history and plan follow-up visits as your dose changes.

Questions to Ask a Prescriber

  • Am I a candidate based on my BMI and health conditions?
  • Which medicine and brand do you recommend, and why?
  • How will my dose be increased, and how often will we follow up?
  • What side effects should I watch for, and when should I call you?
  • Is this covered by my insurance, and what will it cost?
  • What happens if I stop the medication later?

Red Flags With Online Sellers

  • No prescription or medical evaluation required
  • No licensed clinician or state-licensed pharmacy named
  • Products sold "for research use only" or "not for human use"
  • Prices that look far below normal for a brand-name medicine
  • Pressure tactics, countdown timers or "limited supply" claims
  • Promises of a specific amount of weight loss

The FDA has warned consumers about counterfeit GLP-1 products and about unapproved or compounded versions sold online. If something seems off, talk to a licensed clinician or pharmacist before using it.

Published randomized controlled trials show average weight loss of approximately 15% of body weight with semaglutide (STEP-1, NEJM 2021, n=1,961) and about 21% with the highest dose of tirzepatide (SURMOUNT-1, NEJM 2022, n=2,539). Individual results vary significantly with starting weight, adherence, dosing, dietary habits and ongoing clinical support.
GLP-1 receptor agonists work through several mechanisms at once: they activate satiety signals in the hypothalamus to reduce hunger, slow gastric emptying so you feel full longer, stimulate insulin release only when blood sugar is elevated, and suppress glucagon to reduce unnecessary glucose output from the liver. Together these effects reduce calorie intake and improve metabolic function.
Among FDA-approved options, tirzepatide (dual GLP-1 + GIP, sold as Zepbound and Mounjaro) showed about 21% average weight loss at the highest dose in SURMOUNT-1, and semaglutide (GLP-1 only, sold as Wegovy, Ozempic and Rybelsus) showed about 15% in STEP-1. The right medicine for you depends on your health history, current medications, contraindications and your clinician's recommendation.
Many people notice reduced appetite within the first 1–2 weeks. Meaningful weight change is typically visible between weeks 4–12 as doses are increased. In clinical trials, most of the weight loss occurred over the first several months of treatment. This is a gradual process, not a crash diet.
Weight regain is common after stopping GLP-1 medication without lasting lifestyle changes. A 2022 study in Diabetes, Obesity and Metabolism found that participants regained about two-thirds of their lost weight within a year of stopping semaglutide. Nutrition, activity and habit changes built during treatment help, and decisions about stopping or continuing should be made with a licensed clinician.
